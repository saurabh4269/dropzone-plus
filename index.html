Ring × drone delivery

Your Ring camera checks the drone drop zone before the drone arrives.

Amazon wants Prime Air to deliver 500 million packages a year by the end of the decade. Before each drone lands, Ring checks the spot and Alexa tells you what is in the way. With your yes, the pet door lets the dog in, yard lights come on at dusk and the sprinkler waits. The carrier gets a HOLD signal before landing if the spot is not clear.

Nobody checks the drop zone before the drone shows up.

Drone delivery is moving from pilots to everyday use. Amazon Prime Air is expanding across the US, and Wing with Walmart plans 270+ locations. Each delivery needs a clear patch of yard roughly 10×10 ft.

  • Amazon asks customers to keep “people, pets, vehicles, and objects taller than 5 feet … at least 10 feet away from the selected delivery point.” Amazon drone delivery FAQ
  • If anyone runs through the zone, the drone aborts and the item goes by ground instead. A Phoenix customer's advice: “If your dogs are in the backyard, bring them in.” CT Insider · Business Insider
  • Packages have ended up in pools and ponds. The Verge · AZFamily
  • Drop points are chosen remotely from maps, and the drone sees the spot for a few seconds from about 13 ft up. Prime Air technology

A Ring camera has watched that same patch of ground for months. It knows the dog is out right now, that a patio umbrella appeared yesterday, and where the pool is. DropZone uses that knowledge before the drone arrives, while the household can still do something about it.

How it works

From T−8 minutes to the drop, then a receipt afterwards.

1

Ring sees the zone

Ring frames are checked against the zone you drew. The answer is CLEAR, NOT CLEAR or UNKNOWN, timestamped, and expires after 90 seconds.

2

Alexa tells the household

A simulated Alexa+ add-on: “Your drone delivery is about 8 minutes away, and there's a dog in the drop zone. Want me to set the pet door to come-in-only?”

3

Devices help, with your yes

Only after the owner says yes does the simulated pet door go come-in-only. It restores itself after the drone has left.

4

Carrier gets one bit

At T−60 s the carrier sees HOLD (with a reason code) or NO_ADVISORY. The carrier never receives video. After the drop, a Ring frame becomes a proof-of-drop receipt.

Safety and privacy by design

Designed against the Ring Program Requirements. If DropZone is down, slow or unsure, the carrier does exactly what it does today.

Advisory only

The carrier endpoint can only return HOLD or NO_ADVISORY, and authorises_drop is always false. Holds are capped at 3. The aircraft's own detect-and-avoid stays authoritative.

UNKNOWN is first-class

Offline camera, encrypted feed, an error, a stale frame or no zone: the answer is UNKNOWN, never CLEAR.

Labels only

“person”, “dog”, “vehicle”. No face matching, no tracking across locations, no plates. The carrier gets reason codes, never images.

Consent before devices act

The pet door refuses to change without the owner's explicit yes, and restores itself after the drop or after 15 minutes.

Data minimisation

In the browser path, frames are analysed on the device and never leave it. The server keeps only the latest verdict, in memory.

Carrier-neutral

It watches your own yard, not any carrier. It works with any drone operator and monitors no Amazon service's availability or performance.

For delivery partners

One small, read-only HTTP contract. Poll it before the drop. It can ask for a delay; it can never authorise a landing.

  • Two answers only: HOLD (with reason codes such as PERSON_IN_ZONE) or NO_ADVISORY.
  • Fail-open to today: CLEAR and UNKNOWN both map to NO_ADVISORY.
  • Bounded: each advisory carries observed_at and valid_until; holds are capped.
  • No media: no images, no video, no identities ever cross the boundary.

Partners in the app are real drone operators shown as a preview. DropZone is not affiliated with them and no carrier integration exists today.
